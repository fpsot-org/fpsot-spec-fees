= Orders and payments
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A payer may try to pay more than once.
A card can fail, a page can time out, and a bank transfer can take days to arrive.
This page therefore keeps what the payer owes apart from each try to pay it.
A fee order holds what the payer owes, and each payment attempt records one try.
The statuses of an attempt come from GOV.UK Pay, and they move in one direction only.
Card data stays at the provider.

== Fee orders

[[FEE-040]]
*FEE-040.* Each fee order MUST be a record of the class `FeeOrder` of `model/fees.yaml`.
It MUST name its quotes, its payer and the reference that the payer sees.
The amount due MUST equal the sum of the totals of its quotes.
A remission that reduces it under FEE-064 comes off that sum.
An order MUST hold one currency.
An order whose amount due is zero MUST take no payment attempt.

The payer is a `Party` of DMO-040, by id.
The payment reference appears on the page of the provider, in the bank statement and on the receipt.
It MUST NOT hold personal information, under DAT-062.

[[FEE-041]]
*FEE-041.* Each payment attempt MUST be a record of the class `PaymentAttempt`, which names its order.
Its amount MUST equal the amount due of the order.
An order MUST NOT have two attempts that are not finished at the same time.
An order MUST NOT have more than one attempt with the status `success`.
When a second payment succeeds all the same, the engine MUST refund it with the reason `duplicate-payment`.

One order with many attempts follows the payment intent of Stripe.
GOV.UK Pay instead creates a new payment for each try.
The order keeps the link that a payer, a clerk and an auditor look for.

== Statuses

[[FEE-042]]
*FEE-042.* The status of each attempt MUST be one of these eight values.
Its field `finished` MUST match the table.

[cols="1,1,4",options="header"]
|===
|Status |`finished` |Meaning
|`created` |false |The engine created the payment at the provider, and the payer has not reached its page.
|`started` |false |The payer reached the page of the provider.
|`submitted` |false |The payer sent the details, and the provider is still at work or waits for a confirmation.
|`capturable` |false |The provider authorized the payment, and the engine may capture it.
|`success` |true |The provider took the payment.
|`failed` |true |The method was rejected, the payment expired or the payer canceled, and no money moved.
|`cancelled` |true |The service canceled the payment, and no money moved.
|`error` |true |The provider or the engine had a fault, and no money moved.
|===

A finished attempt is not a paid one.
Only `success` pays an order.
A bank transfer can stay `submitted` for days, until the provider or the bank statement shows the credit.

[[FEE-043]]
*FEE-043.* The statuses of an attempt MUST move in the order of the table.
A finished status MUST NOT change.
A read that shows an earlier status MUST NOT move an attempt back.
A status MAY skip steps, such as from `created` to `success`.
An attempt that ends in `failed`, `cancelled` or `error` MUST give a failure code of the model.
The history of the attempt MUST keep each status, with its instant and what the engine read to set it.

The failure codes are `method-rejected`, `expired`, `canceled-by-payer`, `canceled-by-service` and `provider-error`.
A refund or a dispute never changes the status of the attempt.
Each one has its own record, under FEE-060 and FEE-067.

[[FEE-044]]
*FEE-044.* Each attempt MUST keep its settlement apart from its status.
The settlement is `not-settled` or `settled`, with the date and the payout that carried the funds.
An attempt MUST NOT settle before `success`.
An engine MUST NOT treat `success` as proof that the funds reached the account of the body.

GOV.UK Pay also keeps a settlement summary for each payment, apart from its status.
Under FEE-091, the engine then settles each attempt from a payout.

[[FEE-045]]
*FEE-045.* The status of an order MUST follow from its amount and its attempts.
It is `no-payment-due` when the amount due is zero, and `paid` when one attempt succeeded.
Otherwise it is `awaiting-payment`, or `canceled` when the body canceled the order before any payment.
A register keeps the payment status of a filing under REG-024.
It MUST map these values onto the list of its pack.

The pleasure craft pack of the registry specification lists `not-required`, `pending`, `paid` and `exempt`.
An exempt quote gives an order with no payment due, and the filing shows `exempt`.

== Card data

[[FEE-046]]
*FEE-046.* Software that meets this specification MUST NOT receive, store, log or send card data.
Card data is the card number, the security code, the expiry date, the track data and the PIN.
An interface, a record, a log, an event or a receipt MUST NOT hold any of them.
A record MAY show a card by its brand and its last four digits, as the provider returns them.
A record MUST give the method only as a category, such as `card` or `direct-debit`.

Card data in a system of a public body brings that system under the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard.
With none of it, the scope stays at the provider.
The logs follow OBS-090 and OBS-091, and an event holds no amount and no card, under FEE-076.

[[FEE-047]]
*FEE-047.* By default, a card payment MUST use a full redirect to the page of the provider.
The provider then returns the payer to the body.

A full redirect keeps every script of the body away from the payment form.
GOV.UK Pay works this way, and so does the hosted checkout of Stripe.
In February 2025, the PCI Security Standards Council said that its new script criterion does not apply to a redirect.

[[FEE-048]]
*FEE-048.* A body MAY embed the payment form of its provider in its own page, such as in a frame.
It MUST first record how its page meets the script criterion of the Self-Assessment Questionnaire A of January 2025.
The record MUST name the provider, the method, the person who checked it and its date.
The method is either controls on the scripts of the page or a confirmation from the provider.
The record MUST also name the acquirer or the card brand that accepted that method.

The method follows FAQ 1588 of the PCI Security Standards Council, as its notice of 28 February 2025 summarizes it.
The text of the questionnaire and of the FAQ is _to be verified_ at the source.
Reports say that a 2026 revision of FAQ 1331 asks merchants to consult their acquirer.
That revision is _to be verified_ too.

== Provider reads

[[FEE-049]]
*FEE-049.* The return of the payer from the page of the provider MUST NOT change the status of an attempt.
On that return, the engine MUST read the payment at the provider, and set the status from that read.

A payer can open the return address by hand, or a browser can replay it.
The return proves only that a browser arrived.

[[FEE-050]]
*FEE-050.* Each request that creates an order, an attempt or a refund MUST carry an `Idempotency-Key`, under API-060 to API-063.
When the provider accepts a key, the engine MUST send its own key for each attempt and each refund.
The engine MUST NOT send the same key for two different attempts.

A retry after a timeout then finds the first attempt, instead of charging the payer twice.
GOV.UK Pay and Stripe both accept such a key.

[[FEE-051]]
*FEE-051.* An attempt MUST keep the id of its payment at the provider.
It MUST also keep the status that the provider gave at the last read.
Each adapter MUST publish how it maps the statuses of its provider onto the eight statuses of FEE-042.

The raw status lets an auditor check the mapping long after the provider changed its interface.
The page xref:alignment.adoc[Alignment with other standards] gives the mappings for GOV.UK Pay, GovStack, ISO 20022 and Stripe.
