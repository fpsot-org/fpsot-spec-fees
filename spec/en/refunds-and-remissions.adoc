= Refunds, remissions and disputes
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

Three acts undo or soften a payment, and each one has its own cause.
A refund returns money that the body should not keep, such as a fee for a service that never came.
A remission forgives or returns money that the payer owed, by a decision under a law.
A dispute starts at the bank of the payer, outside the control of the body.

Canadian federal law keeps refund and remission apart twice.
Under section 20 of the Financial Administration Act, money paid for a purpose that fails may go back.
The body keeps the part for services already rendered.
Under section 23, the Governor in Council may remit a debt on grounds of equity or the public interest.
Under section 7 of the Service Fees Act, a body that misses a performance standard remits part of the fee.

The remission policy of Environment and Climate Change Canada adds a point.
An overpayment or an administrative error leads to a refund, not a remission.

== Refunds

[[FEE-060]]
*FEE-060.* Each refund MUST be a record of the class `Refund` of `model/fees.yaml`.
It MUST name its order and one attempt of that order with the status `success`.
Its status MUST be `submitted`, `success` or `error`, and the last two are final.
An engine MUST NOT treat `submitted` as the outcome of a refund.

GOV.UK Pay warns that some providers keep a refund `submitted` for a while.
A refund that ends in `error` moved no money, and the body may try again with a new refund.

[[FEE-061]]
*FEE-061.* The refunds of an attempt MUST NOT add up to more than its amount.
A refund in `error` does not count.
Each request for a refund MUST state the refundable balance that the client saw.
The engine MUST refuse the request when that balance differs from its own.
An interface MAY meet the same need with a conditional request on the attempt, under API-008 and API-009.

Two clerks may refund the same payment at the same time.
GOV.UK Pay guards against that with the same echo of the balance.

[[FEE-062]]
*FEE-062.* Each refund MUST give a refund reason of its profile or of `core`.
Some reasons of a profile come with a deduction for services already rendered.
A refund for such a reason MUST state that deduction, even when it is zero.
The reason `purpose-not-fulfilled` MUST serve when the payer paid for a service that the body did not provide.

The federal law keeps the part for services rendered.
It does so in section 20 of the Financial Administration Act and in section 2 of its regulations on repayment.
The `ca-federal` profile therefore ties that reason to a deduction.

[[FEE-063]]
*FEE-063.* A refund SHOULD go back by the method of the payment that it refunds.
A refund by another method MUST name its method and the reason for the change.

A provider may refuse a refund to an expired card.
The body then pays the payer another way, and the record shows why.

== Remissions

[[FEE-064]]
*FEE-064.* Each remission MUST rest on a decision under the decisions specification, with its own decision record.
The record MUST have the outcome `remitted`, the amount remitted, and the legal basis among its legal authorities.
Each remission MUST be a record of the class `Remission`, which names that decision record and its order.
When the payer has paid, the remission MUST lead to refunds that add up to the amount remitted.
When the payer has not paid, it MUST reduce the amount due of its order instead.

The decision holds the facts, such as whether the body met its performance standard in a fiscal year.
The refund only moves the money.

[[FEE-065]]
*FEE-065.* A refund MUST NOT imply a remission.
Only a refund with the reason `remission` MAY name a remission, and such a refund MUST name one.
A report of remissions MUST count remission records only, never refunds.

A federal body reports its remissions to Parliament under section 20 of the Service Fees Act.
It also reports them in the Public Accounts under section 24 of the Financial Administration Act.
A refund for an error that showed up in those reports would mislead both.

[[FEE-066]]
*FEE-066.* A body may remit for many payers at once.
It MUST then make one decision record for each payer and each order.
It MUST NOT cover several payers with one record.

A remission for a missed performance standard reaches every payer of the fiscal year.
Each record then gives the reasons for one payer, under DEC-052.

== Disputes

[[FEE-067]]
*FEE-067.* Each dispute MUST be a record of the class `Dispute`, which names its attempt.
Its status MUST be `needs-response`, `under-review`, `won` or `lost`, and the last two are final.
A dispute in `needs-response` MUST give the last day to send evidence.

The four statuses come from GOV.UK Pay.
A dispute never changes the status of its attempt, which stays `success`.

[[FEE-068]]
*FEE-068.* An engine MUST NOT create a refund on an attempt while a dispute on that attempt is open.
A dispute is open from its opening until it reaches `won` or `lost`.

A refund while the bank reviews a dispute could pay the payer twice.
GOV.UK Pay lists a dispute reason for a refund that the payer expected and did not get.

[[FEE-069]]
*FEE-069.* A lost dispute MUST appear in reconciliation as a payout line of the kind `dispute-reversal`.
Its fee MUST appear as a line of the kind `dispute-fee`.
An engine MUST NOT record a lost dispute as a refund.

A provider may charge a fee for a dispute, and some charge it whether the body wins or loses.
The fee then reduces the next payout, under FEE-091.
