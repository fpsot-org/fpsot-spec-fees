= Schedules and quotes
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A fee is a decision.
The registry specification asks this of every register under REG-026.
The decisions specification already keeps amounts in dated parameter files, under DEC-037.
This page adds what a fee needs beyond them.
Those are the fee items, the indexation steps, rounding, exemptions, taxes and the lines of a quote.

== Fee schedules

[[FEE-020]]
*FEE-020.* Each fee schedule MUST be a file of the class `FeeSchedule` of `model/fees.yaml`.
That class extends the `ParameterSet` of DEC-037, so each amount stays a dated parameter value.
The schedule MUST name its profile.
Each fee item MUST have a code, a name in both languages and a basis.
It MUST also have a legal source, a tax treatment, and an amount parameter or bands that each name one.
A schedule that changes MUST take a new version.

Each decision record names the version of the schedule and its digest, under DEC-037.
The digest ties every quote to the exact file that priced it.
A changed amount under the same version would break that link, and the check of the examples rejects it.

[[FEE-021]]
*FEE-021.* Each amount of a schedule MUST be money under DAT-050 to DAT-053.
It has the decimals of the minor unit of its currency.
Each rate MUST be a decimal string in percent, such as `"2.05"`, under DAT-041.
An engine MUST NOT compute with binary floating-point numbers, under DAT-055.

[[FEE-022]]
*FEE-022.* The basis of each fee item MUST be one of these values.
A fee item whose basis is not `flat` MUST name the unit of its quantity, under DAT-043.

* `flat`: one amount, whatever the quantity.
* `per-unit`: an amount for each unit.
* `tiered`: each band of the quantity pays the amount of its own band.
* `banded`: the whole quantity pays the amount of the band that it falls in.
* `percentage`: a rate on a stated value.

A tiered or banded fee item MUST list its bands, each with its own amount parameter.
Each band gives its first quantity and the first quantity after it, and the last band has no end.
A percentage line MUST give the rate in force and the base value that the rate applies to.

The difference between `tiered` and `banded` changes the amount at each edge of a band.
The federal schedule of the examples has both, with a surcharge of 10% on a base value.
Four hours of inspection, tiered at $120.00 for the first three and $90.00 after, cost $450.00.
Twelve samples, banded at $25.00 each from ten samples, cost $300.00.
A schedule that names the basis lets a reviewer check the amount without reading code.

== Indexation

[[FEE-023]]
*FEE-023.* An indexed amount MUST be a dated value of the schedule.
Each parameter MUST have one value for each date.
An engine MUST NOT compute an indexed amount at run time from an index, a rate or a formula.

A published value lets a lawyer review the number that the payer sees.
It also keeps each decision a function of its input and its parameters, as DEC-023 asks.
In Québec, the body that fixed a fee publishes each indexed result.
Section 83.6 of the Loi sur l'administration financière says so.

[[FEE-024]]
*FEE-024.* Each indexed value MUST come with an indexation step in the schedule, of the class `IndexationStep`.
The step MUST name the indexation rule of the profile, the published rate and where the rate appeared.
It MUST also give the rate after any cap and the amount before the step.
It MUST give the result before rounding, whether the profile deferred it, and the result.
A rule that carries changes forward or defers them MUST state the accumulated rate.
That rate MUST be the applied rate, plus the accumulated rate of a previous step that the rule deferred.

Anyone can then redo the arithmetic from the file alone.
Under section 17 of the Service Fees Act, a change below 1% carries forward.
The rule then defers a step at 0.6%, and a next step at 0.7% applies 1.3%.

For 2026, the published Québec rate is 2.05%, below the cap of 3%.
A fee of $87.00 then gives 88.7835, which the regulation rounds to $88.75.
A fee of $2.00 would rise by less than $0.05.
It therefore stays $2.00, and the rate of 2.05% carries forward.

The cap law of Québec excepts the fees of the regulations in its Schedule I.
A fee item of such a regulation carries a flag that lifts the cap, under FEE-012.
The research read only an archived copy of the cap law from 2024.
The law and its list are therefore _to be verified_.

[[FEE-025]]
*FEE-025.* Each schedule MUST name the rounding rule of its profile that its quotes and their taxes follow.
That rule MUST round to the cent, unless a law that the profile cites sets another rounding for quotes.
The schedule MUST also state the stage at which the engine rounds a quote: `per-line` or `per-total`.
Each rounding rule MUST state its method and its step for each band of results, under DAT-054.
It MUST also state where a result halfway goes, and whether it rounds quotes, indexed values or both.

Where a law sets the rounding, the profile MUST cite it, and the rule MUST follow it.
An indexed value MUST round under the rule that its indexation rule names.
When that rule names none, the indexed value MUST round under the rule of `core`.

The Québec regulation rounds only tariffs indexed under sections 83.3 and 83.4 of the Loi sur l'administration financière.
It rounds to 0.05 below $10 and to 0.10 below $25.
It rounds to 0.25 below $100, and to the dollar above.
A result halfway goes up.
Its bands therefore round indexed values only, and a Québec quote and its taxes round to the cent.

The Service Fees Act lets a federal body round an adjusted fee down, under Treasury Board policy.
That policy is _to be verified_.

== Quotes

[[FEE-026]]
*FEE-026.* A quote MUST be a decision under the decisions specification, with one decision record under DEC-040.
The record MUST name the schedule in its parameter sets, with its digest, under DEC-037.
Its amount MUST be the total due, and its outcome a code such as `fee-payable`, `exempt` or `partly-exempt`.
An engine MUST NOT price a fee outside a decision record.

The record then holds the facts, the rule version, the parameters and the reasons of the price.
A replay under DEC-047 gives the same amount.

[[FEE-027]]
*FEE-027.* The lines of a quote MUST be in a record of the class `FeeQuote` of `model/fees.yaml`.
That record names the decision record.
Each line MUST name its fee item, its quantity, its legal source and the unit amount in force.
It MUST also give the first date of that value, the gross amount, any exemption, the amount and the taxes.
The total MUST equal both the sum of the lines and taxes and the amount of the decision record.
A quote MUST NOT change once stored.

The decision record has no field for line detail, and the decisions specification keeps that class closed.
The quote therefore carries the lines, and the record carries the result.
The receipt of FEE-081 copies the lines, so a payer sees each fee with its source.

[[FEE-028]]
*FEE-028.* Each exemption that a quote applies MUST be one that its fee item grants.
The fee item names its legal source and the fact which triggers it.
That fact MUST be an input of the decision record, with its source, under DEC-042.
A fact that the payer declares MUST keep the source `applicant-declaration`, and MUST NOT become a status that someone verified.
A discretionary waiver MUST lead to the result kind `referred`, under DEC-026.
A person then decides it, under DEC-061 to DEC-063.

Section 112 of the Small Vessel Regulations sets a fee of $24 for a pleasure craft license.
An owner who declares a right on the application pays nothing.
The right is one under section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982.
The engine records that declaration as a fact, and the quote applies the exemption.
The file `examples/decisions/pleasure-craft-quote.json` shows that record.

SOR/2025-272 replaced that section, and the research did not read its text in full.
The amount and the exemption are therefore _to be verified_.

[[FEE-029]]
*FEE-029.* Each line MUST state how each tax that its profile names applies, with its legal source.
The treatment is `taxable` with a rate and an amount, `exempt`, `zero-rated` or `outside-scope`.
A tax that is not `taxable` MUST have an amount of zero.
A tax MUST round under the rule of the schedule, under FEE-025.
That rule rounds to the cent, unless a cited law says otherwise.

The goods and services tax does not apply to a license or a permit that a government supplies.
Schedule V of the Excise Tax Act exempts them.
It also exempts the processing of an application for them.
Some rights stay taxable, such as hunting and fishing rights.
How the Québec sales tax applies to Québec fees is _to be verified_.
The profile `ca-qc` therefore names no such tax yet.

[[FEE-030]]
*FEE-030.* A quote MUST state the last instant at which an order may use it.
An order after that instant MUST rest on a new quote and a new decision.
A penalty or interest for late payment MUST be a line of its own decision.
That decision follows a rule of the profile.
An engine MUST NOT add such an amount to an order outside a decision.

Each rate that such a rule applies, such as an interest rate, MUST be a dated value of the profile.
That value follows the terms of FEE-023.
An engine MUST NOT compute that rate at run time from a reference rate.

The `ca-federal` profile holds the interest rule of the Interest and Administrative Charges Regulations.
Interest there is the average bank rate plus 3%, compounded monthly.
The average bank rate of a month is the weighted average of the bank rates of the month before.
The Bank Rate stayed at 2.50% from 30 October 2025 to 28 October 2026.
The profile therefore publishes 5.50% for each month from December 2025 to October 2026.

The Bank Rate changed on 29 October 2026.
The value for November 2026 therefore waits for the next release of the profile.
Until then, an interest decision for that month returns `not-applicable`, under FEE-013.
Nobody can explain or replay a penalty that no decision records.
