= Jurisdiction profiles
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

The fees that the research compared share their mechanics and split on every legal value.
The federal Service Fees Act adjusts most fees each year by the consumer price index of April.
It skips a change below 1% and carries it to the next year.
Québec indexes its fees each 1 January by its own index, and rounds the result by bands.
From 2023 to 2026, a Québec law caps that rate at 3%.
In France, a decree creates a charge for a service, and the next finance act must ratify it.

No single rule could serve all of them.
This page therefore keeps each legal value and each legal rule in a jurisdiction profile, apart from the core rules.
A profile states the practice of {abbr-fpsot-first}, and it is not legal advice.

== Profile files

[[FEE-010]]
*FEE-010.* Each {term-deploying-body} MUST name the profiles whose law applies to its fees.
Each profile MUST be a file of the class `FeeProfile` of `model/fees.yaml`, in the folder `profiles/`.
Each profile MUST list its sources.
For each source, it MUST state whether the research read it, or mark it `to-be-verified`.
A source read in an archived copy counts as read only when that copy holds the text in force.
The profile then gives that copy.

[cols="2,3,4",options="header"]
|===
|Profile |Applies to |Content of this release
|`core` |Every fee |The defaults of FEE-014 and the refund reasons that every engine knows.
|`ca-federal` |Fees of federal bodies of Canada |The yearly adjustment by the consumer price index, the remission for a missed performance standard, the refund of a purpose not fulfilled, the low-materiality thresholds, interest with its monthly rates, the charges for a dishonored payment and the exemption from the goods and services tax.
|`ca-qc` |Fees of Québec public bodies |The indexation of each 1 January, its cap until 2026 with the flag that lifts it for the fees of Schedule I of the cap law, the deferral of fees below $5 and the rounding bands of indexed values.
|===

A French profile does not exist in this release.
The research read no French code at its source, so `jurisdictions/fr.yaml` describes France without a normative profile.

The `ca-federal` profile follows the Service Fees Act, the Financial Administration Act and its regulations, as the research read them.
A body that misses a performance standard remits part of the fee, under section 7 of the Service Fees Act.
It must do so before 1 July of the next fiscal year.
Under section 17, the adjustment follows the consumer price index of April, and a change below 1% carries forward.
The directive of the {abbr-tbs-first} on charging refused automated readers, so its rules are _to be verified_.

The `ca-qc` profile follows chapter VIII.1 of the Loi sur l'administration financière.
It also follows the rounding regulation and the law that caps indexation.
The research read the act and the regulation in archived copies of LégisQuébec, which hold the texts in force.
The only archived copy of the cap law dates from 2024.
The government may remove a regulation from its Schedule I, so the cap law is _to be verified_.

A fee below $5 waits until the sum of the deferred rates raises it by $0.05.
On 2026-11-21, the research found no law that extends the cap past 2026.

== Sources and dates

[[FEE-011]]
*FEE-011.* Each rule and each value of a profile MUST cite one of the sources of that profile.
Each value MUST carry the dates to which it applies.
Each profile MUST have its own version number under Semantic Versioning 2.0.0, apart from the version of this specification.
A release that removes or relaxes a value of the same profile MUST take a new major version.

Fee law changes each year, and the cap of Québec ends on 31 December 2026.
A new rate is then a profile release, not a change to the rules.
The decisions specification dates its parameters in the same way, under DEC-037.

== No law in the core

[[FEE-012]]
*FEE-012.* An engine MUST take each rate, threshold, deadline and rounding rule from a profile or a fee schedule.
It MUST take each refund reason and tax treatment from them too.
It MUST NOT hard-code one.
A flag of a fee item, such as `low-materiality`, MUST only select rules of its profile.
It MUST NOT change a value.
A profile MUST NOT relax a MUST of the core rules.

A schedule holds the fees of one body, and a profile holds the law that many bodies share.
The same engine then serves a federal office and a Québec body, each with its own profile.

== Missing values

[[FEE-013]]
*FEE-013.* A rule may need a value that neither the profile, the schedule nor `core` gives for the effective date.
The rule provider MUST then return the result kind `not-applicable` of DEC-024.
Its reason code MUST name the missing value.
The engine MUST NOT create an order from that result.
It MUST NOT assume a value.

A silent default would turn a gap in a profile into a fee that nobody set.
The pleasure craft schedule of the examples ends its value on 1 April 2026 for that reason.
The research did not read the value that the yearly adjustment sets on that date.

== Defaults

[[FEE-014]]
*FEE-014.* Each fee MUST also follow the `core` profile, which sets these defaults.

* Card payments use a full redirect to the page of the provider, under FEE-047.
* The engine seals each receipt at least under the profile `seal-log` of the signed records specification, under FEE-082.
* When no law sets a rounding, amounts round to the cent, and a result halfway goes up.
The schedule states that rule, as DAT-054 asks.
* The refund reasons `overpayment`, `duplicate-payment`, `administrative-error`, `purpose-not-fulfilled` and `remission` exist in every profile.

A jurisdiction profile MAY replace a refund reason of `core` that has the same code.
The `ca-federal` profile replaces `purpose-not-fulfilled` to tie it to its law.
Under that law, the body keeps the part of the fee for services already rendered.
